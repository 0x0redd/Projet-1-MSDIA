\section{Statistical Validation}
\label{sec:stats}

A single accuracy number on one test split is weak evidence: it could be luck. This stage establishes
whether the observed differences between models are \textbf{real, stable, and reproducible}. Six
complementary tests are applied, moving from per-model agreement to rigorous multi-classifier
comparison.

\subsection{Cohen's Kappa ($\kappa$)}
Measures agreement between predictions and ground truth \textbf{beyond chance}, correcting for class
imbalance (so it is stricter than accuracy). Interpretation: $<0.20$ slight, $0.20$--$0.40$ fair,
$0.40$--$0.60$ moderate, $0.60$--$0.80$ substantial, $>0.80$ almost perfect. Computed for every
(feature-set $\times$ model) on the test split.

\subsection{Bootstrap Confidence Intervals (95\,\%)}
The test predictions are resampled with replacement $N=2{,}000$ times; the 2.5th and 97.5th
percentiles of the resulting metric distribution form a \textbf{95\,\% confidence interval} around
accuracy and macro-F1. Non-overlapping intervals between two models are strong evidence of a genuine
difference.

\subsection{Cross-validation fold statistics}
The mean $\pm$ standard deviation of macro-F1 across the CV folds quantifies \textbf{stability}: a
model with a high mean but large variance is less trustworthy than a slightly lower but consistent
one.

\subsection{McNemar's test (pairwise, same test set)}
The standard significance test for two classifiers evaluated on the \textbf{same} samples. It builds
a contingency table of where the two models disagree (A right/B wrong vs.\ A wrong/B right) and tests,
with continuity correction, whether those disagreements are balanced. $p<0.05$ means the two
classifiers make significantly different errors. Run across all model pairs on the best feature set,
\texttt{Full(opt)}.

\subsection{Wilcoxon signed-rank test (paired, across folds)}
A non-parametric paired test comparing two classifiers' per-fold macro-F1 scores. It is preferred
over a paired $t$-test because, with only five folds, normality cannot be assumed. $H_0$: the median
per-fold difference is zero (the models are equivalent).

\subsection{Friedman test + Nemenyi post-hoc}
The gold standard for comparing \textbf{many} classifiers over multiple folds (Dem\v{s}ar, 2006). The
Friedman test (a non-parametric repeated-measures ANOVA) first asks whether \textbf{any} classifier
differs from the others; if it is significant ($p<0.05$), the \textbf{Nemenyi post-hoc} test
identifies exactly which pairs differ while controlling for multiple comparisons.
